Residual Plot

Residuals are the vertical distances between each data point and the fitted line. Random scatter = good fit. Patterns = a problem.

What to look for: Residuals should be randomly scattered around zero with no fan-shape (homoscedasticity) and no curves (linearity assumption).

Key Takeaways

Understanding correlation is essential before building any predictive model.

r = +1 is a perfect positive relationship

As X increases, Y increases proportionally. E.g. hours studied vs. exam score.

r = 0 means no linear relationship

The variables may still have a non-linear relationship — always plot your data!

Correlation ≠ Causation

Two variables can be correlated due to a third confounding variable. Establishing causation requires experiments or causal inference methods.

Small n → noisy estimates

With only 20 points, measured r can differ greatly from the true population r. Increase n to see estimates stabilise.

4 · The Data Science Workflow

Every data science project follows a similar lifecycle. Understanding this workflow helps you stay organised and produce reproducible results.

1 · Define the Problem

Translate a business or research question into a concrete, measurable goal. What variable are you predicting? What counts as success?

KPIs Metrics Stakeholders

2 · Collect & Clean Data

Real-world data is messy. You will spend 60–80 % of project time on data wrangling: handling missing values, outliers, and type errors.

EDA Missing values Outliers

3 · Explore & Visualise

Plot distributions, correlations, and time trends before modelling. Visualisation reveals patterns that summary statistics hide.

Histograms Scatter plots Box plots

4 · Feature Engineering

Transform raw variables into informative inputs for your model: encoding categoricals, scaling numerics, creating interaction terms.

Normalisation One-hot encoding PCA

5 · Model & Evaluate

Train a model, tune hyperparameters, and rigorously evaluate on held-out data. Always use cross-validation to avoid overfitting.

Train/Test split Cross-validation Bias–Variance

6 · Communicate Results

A model no one understands will never be used. Visualise predictions, quantify uncertainty, and tell a clear story with your findings.

Storytelling Uncertainty Reproducibility
